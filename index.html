The Premise

Cells whisper in photons

Nearly a century ago, Alexander Gurwitsch observed that living cells emit ultraweak photon emissions (UPE) — vanishingly faint flickers of light produced by the biochemical reactions that power life.

10−11 W/m²

Power flux density of photon emissions from melanoma cells — far below human vision, but measurable with photomultiplier tubes.

100 seconds

That's all the measurement time needed. A brief photon recording can reveal the spectral fingerprint of malignancy.

90% Accuracy

Spectral Power Density analysis of photon emissions discriminated malignant from non-malignant cells with up to 90% accuracy.

"Photons are the bases of changes of energies across electron orbits in the atomic and molecular reactions that drive living systems."
The Experiment

How do you listen to light?

01

Cell Cultures

13 cell lines — human pancreatic, breast, cervical, kidney, and mouse melanoma — both malignant and non-malignant, each cultured to ~1 million cells.

02

Photomultiplier Detection

Plates placed over digital PMT apertures inside light-tight black boxes. Photons counted every 20 ms (50 Hz sampling) for 100 seconds.

03

Mouse Model

47 C57 male mice: half injected with B16-BL6 melanoma cells, controls received UV-killed cells or no injection. Whole-body photons measured over 21 days.

04

Spectral Analysis

Fast Fourier Transform converts raw photon counts into Spectral Power Density profiles (0.01–25 Hz), revealing hidden temporal patterns.

In Vivo Results

Detection within 24 hours

The photon emission profiles of mice injected with melanoma cells diverged from controls as early as one day post-injection — long before any tumor was palpable.

The Discovery

The ~20 Hz signature

Across both cell cultures and tumor-bearing mice, one frequency band stood out: ~20–22 Hz. This spectral power peak appeared consistently in malignant conditions.

In Cell Cultures

When all malignant cell lines were pooled against all non-malignant lines, 22 Hz was the only SPD increment that reached statistical significance.

In Living Mice

Mice with fully developed tumors (day 19) showed significantly higher SPD at 22.6 Hz vs. controls (p = 0.042, η² = 0.68 — a very large effect).

"The power output of a radio generating static or a broadcast message is the same. The difference is the pattern of expression."
Why It Matters

From bench to bedside?

These findings point toward a future where cancer detection could be as simple as measuring light — non-invasively, quickly, and inexpensively.

Early Detection

Photon emission changes detectable within 24 hours of tumor cell injection — far earlier than physical examination.

Non-Invasive

No biopsies, no radiation, no contrast agents. Just a photon detector and 100 seconds of measurement time.

Spectral Fingerprinting

Different malignancies show distinct SPD profiles — potentially enabling tumor-type classification, not just detection.

Therapeutic Link

The ~20 Hz signature aligns with the frequency of weak magnetic fields shown to inhibit cancer cell growth — hinting at a diagnostic-therapeutic bridge.
